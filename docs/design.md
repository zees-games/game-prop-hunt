# Design: the decisions this game rests on

What this game decides, and why the obvious alternative is wrong. The one-line rules are in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).

## What this game is, versus the others

The third asymmetric one, after buses and deathrun, and the first where **one side's whole move is to stop moving**. A prop's skill is choosing what to be and where; a hunter's is reading a room for the thing that is slightly wrong, and the decoy charge is what makes that a skill rather than spraying every chair.

## Decision 1: a disguise is a hull, not a look

Becoming a chair changes what the controller sweeps, what the hitbox is and how much health there is, all as functions of the chair's measured size (`DotPropDisguise` in dot-props, under `DotPropDisguiseRules`): a footprint half as wide as the prop so a disguised player still fits through a door, a height clamped between 0.3 and 2.2 m, health `190 × volume^0.45` between 10 and 200, and a hurt prop that changes shape keeps its share of health. `PhPlayer.apply_disguise` writes the hull **into the controller's tunables in place**, because the motor holds that object by reference and a new one would never reach it.

**Both ends must agree, so both ends compute it from the same number.** A client predicting itself as a chair with a different capsule from the server's walks through a doorway the server's chair does not fit and is pulled back every tick. The size comes from `props/catalogue.json`, measured once offline by `tools/measure_props.gd`, because a headless server has the meshes but no business loading 163 of them to read their bounds. A DISGUISE event carries the prop, its turn and its lock; the receiving end rebuilds the hull from the catalogue. `headless_net` asserts the capsule and health equal on both ends and a running chair within 2 mm of the server's.

**A hull changes size where the player stands, so the server looks for room first** (`PhGame._make_room`): the new capsule where they are, then a step of 0.3 or 0.6 m in eight directions, never through a wall and never off a floor, and otherwise a refusal ("There is no room…"). Without it a bottle by a wall that became a bookcase, or a small prop under a table that showed its face, was a capsule inside the wall or the table, which the motor resolves differently on each machine. Found in review (2026-10-09); `headless_run`'s "a bigger hull needs room" (armed by turning the check off: three fail).

**Turning** is yaw (free, or locked with C so looking around does not turn you), tilt and lean in 15° steps up to 180° (`max_tilt`), so a prop goes every way up: on either side, on its back, on its nose; and upright again. A lock or a tilt is the server's, asked for and announced.

**A lean turns the prop about the middle of its box, and the box rests on the floor** (dot-props' `DotPropDisguise.standing_size`, `standing_hull`, `model_transform`, `box_transform`). Christian's first play test asked to lay a water bottle on each side or its nose "WITHOUT going into the ground (since that'd be cheating)". The first build turned the model about the middle of its footprint on the floor, so a bottle laid down was half under the floor, out of every shot, and the hull and hitbox stayed the upright bottle's. Now the hull is the leant box's (a bottle on its side is low and long), the hitbox and the drawn model turn about the box's middle at half the leant height, and a lean that changes the hull goes through `_make_room` like a new disguise ("There is no room to turn it that way here"). `headless_run`'s "every prop turned every way up" turns every prop a player may become six ways and holds the lowest point of its real meshes to the floor (armed: the old transform puts a prop 2.16 m under it).

## Decision 2: the blindfold is true on the wire

The HUD paints a hunter's screen black during the hide, and a modifier (`PhController.BLINDFOLD`) holds them still on exactly the ticks the server does. Neither stops a client that skips the paint. So `net/ph_interest.gd` sends a hunter's snapshots **no prop at all** during the hide, evaluated every snapshot with no linger: a prop that kept being sent for a second after the hide began would be a second of where everybody ran. `headless_net` moves a prop on the server during the hide and asserts the hunter's copy did not move, then that it is right the tick seeking starts. **What the props became is held back the same way**: a DISGUISE event goes to every peer except a hunter during the hide, and each hunter is sent all of them when the seek starts (`PhNetBridge._on_disguise_changed`, `_on_phase_changed`). Without that a blindfolded client knew "two chairs and a plant", which is half of finding them; found in review, armed (`headless_net`'s "nor what it became").

## Decision 3: a wrong guess costs, and the furniture decides what is wrong

A shot whose impacts land inside a map prop's box, and not on a player, is a decoy: the hunter takes `decoy_penalty` (20%) of the shot's damage, at least `decoy_penalty_min` (1), per pellet that hit furniture (`PhGame._charge_for_decoys`). Death by decoy blows the hunter apart (`hunter_break`, dot-player-char's `DotPlayerBodyBreak`). The client flashes on a DECOY event. **The first stand-in hunter killed itself this way in its first round**: it fired at a prop it could see through a sofa, every pellet hit the sofa, and `dedicated` reported "the hunters are gone" two seconds into the seek. A stand-in now needs a line of sight clear of furniture too, and below 40% health tests only what it is nearly sure of. How stand-ins hide, search and test is [bots.md](bots.md).

## Breakable furniture

Christian's first play test: "props should be breakable and more should be added". Which pieces break, and at how much damage, is `props/kits.json`'s `breaks` (by model pattern: all the food and glass, lamps, screens, radios, plants, books, boxes, bins; never a sofa or a bookcase), measured into `props/catalogue.json` by `tools/measure_props.gd`. `PhGame._break_furniture` adds up the damage of every pellet that landed on a breakable piece and not on a player, after `_charge_for_decoys`: **breaking is not a free guess**, the shots that broke a bottle cost the hunter as a sofa's would. At its number `PhMap.break_prop` disables the piece's collision shapes and hides its drawn instances (a zero-scaled `MultiMesh` instance), so nothing collides with it, hits it or charges for it again; `prop_containing` skips it. A BROKEN event (index, direction, quiet) tells every client; a joiner is told every broken piece quietly after the MAP. A new round restores everything on the server and, on the ROUND event, on every client. A client throws debris: the piece's own meshes as loose bodies and a few chips (`ph_debris.gd`, four seconds, colliding with the building only, so nothing the server does not know of can be stood on).

The catalogue grew from 163 to 204 props: a chosen few of Kenney's Food Kit (bottles, cartons, pots, fruit, plates, mugs) and Survival Kit (crates, barrels, buckets, a water bottle and jug, a workbench, a tent, a bedroll, firewood), each with its licence beside it, placed by mg-prop-hunt-maps where they belong (lunch on the cafeteria's tables, water bottles on desks and gym benches, a camp in the woods, a workbench in the garage, crates in the store rooms).

## Decision 4: taunts are a choice with a price, and standing still is not free

`DotPropTaunts` (dot-props): **taunts start with the seek** (a taunt during the hide was no risk to the prop, and a hunter's client, told nothing about where the props are, played it from where it last saw them). A taunt plays from where the prop stands, to everybody within `taunt_range`, and pays `taunt_points`. A prop that has not moved `auto_taunt_radius` in `auto_taunt_seconds` (60 by default, as game-hide-n-seek; 45 was too soon in Christian's first play test; `ph_auto_taunt_seconds`) is taunted for, at `forced_taunt_points`; the meter on the HUD fills toward it, and a voluntary taunt resets it. The list is `taunts/taunts.json`, which an owner edits.

## Decision 5: the end of a round finds the last props

With `beacon_props_left` props left (1) or `beacon_seconds_left` seconds to go (0, off), every live prop pings: a sound and an expanding ring everybody sees (`DotFxBeacon`, dot-fx), every `beacon_period`. **The time beacon is off by default**: at 30 s it gave every hunter a walk to every prop still in, and the props won 4 rounds in 20 at medium, most lost in the last half minute; off, 11 in 24 ([bots](bots.md#measured)). The last prop's beacon is what still ends a round nobody can find anybody in.

## Toys: loose ones only, never a hiding place

Every map places a few of dot-props' loose toys (the document's `toys`, `PhMap._build_toys`): balls, a tyre, barrels, in the gardens and car parks. Never a fixed one: a swing or a trampoline would be cover, or a way up to a hiding place the map never had, and nobody can disguise as a toy. A person walks through a loose toy and pushes it (`PhGame._advance_toys`); a hunter's shot down the same ray pushes it and goes on to the combat (`_shoot_toys`, dot-props' `shot_along`), whose trace never sees a toy, so it is never cover and never a decoy a hunter pays for. Every toy goes home at a round's start. **The blindfold covers them too**: `PhInterest` sends a hunter no toy during the hide, because a ball rolling off where a prop just walked would be that prop on the wire; it arrives as it stands when the seek starts. Replicated as `net/ph_toy_net.gd` through TOYS after MAP; a client registers every mirror the moment TOYS arrives and hands each its toy when its map is built, because dot-net stops reading a snapshot at the first entity a client does not know.

## A hunter's loadout and aiming

**B is a hunter's loadout** (dot-menu's `DotMenuLoadout` over dot-loadout's `DotLoadoutCommand`, as in the arena): a primary and a secondary, as cards with zee's numbers and the weapon silhouettes in `assets/weapon_icons/` (game-arena's renders). The schema is `game/ph_loadout.gd`: the defaults are the config's `hunter_weapons` (the SMG and the pistol, so a hunter who never presses B plays as before), the alternatives `hunter_choices` (carbine and shotgun; revolver and machine pistol). The hatchet is not a choice: every hunter carries the config's melee. Solo the client asks the world's own command; connected the server's (`/loadout` and the `loadout_menu` notice), and the module binds the owner's `mp_loadout_menu`, `mp_loadout_allow` and `mp_loadout_fixed` (an example is in `game.yml`). **A choice goes into the hands at once only during the hide** (`PhGame.rearm_if_allowed`): the hunter is blindfolded and has fired nothing, so nothing is gained; in the seek it would be a free reload mid-search, so it waits for the next round (`PhGame.arm_hunter` at the draw). A prop may choose too, for the round they hunt. The kit's primary is armed first, because the first weapon armed is the one a client draws. The choice is remembered on the device and sent again on the next join (`PhClient.remember_loadout`, off in suites and renders). The blindfold says "B  Choose your loadout"; a hunter's hints name B.

**A grenade is the optional third slot** (`thrown`, empty by default, so the stand-ins and a hunter who never presses B carry none): zee's frag or sticky from `hunter_choices`, slot five. See "Grenades" below.

**The right mouse button aims down the sights** (`PhBindings.AIM`, the family's Aim row): zee's `ZeeViewModel.aim`, the lens zoomed from `PhClient.base_fov` (the menu's field of view binds there, so a zoom never compounds), the dot faded by dot-ui's `DotCrosshair.opacity_for_aim` and a `ZeeScopeOverlay` through a scoped gun. Presentation only, as in the arena. zee's quick melee, which was the right button, is on F (`PhBindings.BASH`). `tools/shot.sh --view=hunter --aim` and `--view=loadout` render both.

## Grenades

**Flown by the game, as in the arena.** dot-weapon spawns nothing: a thrown weapon's use returns a `DotWeaponSpawn`, and `game/ph_projectiles.gd` (game-arena's `ArenaProjectiles`, copied with its reasons) flies it on the tick: a swept segment against dot-combat's trace (the walls and the furniture, so a frag bounces off a sofa as a shot stops on it), a bounce that keeps more speed along a surface than into it, a sticky that stops on what it touches and rides a prop it hit. It goes off on its fuse and dot-combat resolves the blast as a shot with a splash, so falloff, self damage and friendly fire (off) are every gun's rules. Server side and solo only; a grenade passes through toys, as a shot does.

**On the wire it is one event.** The flight is a pure function of its start, so `PhNetBridge` broadcasts one LAUNCH per throw (the server's number for it, the weapon, the thrower's session, origin and velocity as full floats, gravity, fuse; a client owns its copy by its own entity number for the thrower, or the copy hits the thrower's head leaving the hand) and a client flies its own copy in `client_tick`; a copy resolves nothing (its combat manager is not the authority) and only says where it went off, which is where the client draws the burst (`PhGame.blast_drawn`: game-arena's explosion scene, a low synthesised bang) and the grenade itself (`ph_projectile_view.gd`, zee's model with a red fuse light). Hunters throw only in the seek, so a LAUNCH never crosses the blindfold. `headless_net` checks the copy flies where the server's does, tick for tick, and goes off there.

**What a blast costs (decided 2026-10-13).** A shot that lands in furniture costs per pellet; a blast cannot be split into pellets, so `PhGame.blast_cost` decides it whole:

- **It hurt a prop: nothing.** It found somebody, as a shot that lands on a prop costs nothing.
- **It found nobody and a piece of the map's furniture was within its radius, in clear sight of where it went off: one decoy at the blast's full damage**, `decoy_penalty` of it (at least `decoy_penalty_min`): a frag 23 health, a sticky 27 at the default 0.2. One, however many pieces it reached: a blast is one guess about one place, and a charge per piece would make a grenade in a classroom of thirty chairs a death for testing anything. It is dearer than one wrong pellet because it tests a whole corner at once.
- **Nothing near (an empty yard, a wall): nothing**, as a shot into a wall costs nothing.

The cost goes through the same path as a decoy (`PhGame._charge`: DECOY to the hunter, the furniture's death if it finishes them). A blast also throws every toy in its radius (`DotPropToyBox.blast`, 12 m/s at the centre, the arena's number) and gives the thrower the hit marker for a prop it hurt. It does not break furniture: breaking adds up shot damage per piece, and a blast that broke a classroom's worth of bottles at once would be a tool for clearing rooms rather than testing them. `headless_run` throws a real frag at a prop, and sets one off by the bookcase and one in the open garden.

## A death

**A person falls as dot-player-char's ragdoll; a prop is a prop, not a person, and bursts.** The server picks every death's fall once (`PhGame.decide_death`: DotPlayerDeath over the `sv_ragdoll_reactions` weights, the hunter's gun as a weapon def, so a shotgun may launch; the furniture's cost and a fall push nothing), sets it on `player.death`, and DEATH carries it last and optional. A client then draws what it was drawing (`PhClient._fall`, from `_on_death`, solo and connected):

- **A hunter, or a prop out of disguise**: a ragdoll copied from the figure, in the client's `ragdolls` yard under the server's `sv_ragdoll*` rules (bound REPLICATED by the module and followed through the link), landing on the building and the furniture.
- **A hunter the furniture finished** still comes apart (`hunter_break`, dot-player-char's `DotPlayerBodyBreak`, the brief's own look); with `hunter_break` 0 they ragdoll like anybody else.
- **A disguised prop that is found** pops: its own meshes thrown the way the shot went and shrinking to nothing in a third of a second, in a spray of chips of its colour (`PhClient._burst_prop` through `ph_debris.gd`, the debris shot-up furniture throws, with `pop_seconds` set). Most of Kenney's furniture is one mesh, so thrown whole a found bookcase was a bookcase knocked over, which is what the shrink is for. A ragdoll of a bookcase is a bookcase falling over, which reads as a piece of furniture knocked, not as a player out; a person appearing where the prop was would show a body nobody saw hiding. The burst says "that was a player" in the game's own language, and leaves nothing on the floor a hunter could take for furniture. `prop_death` 1 topples the prop as one stiff piece instead (dot-player-char's one-piece body, under the same `sv_ragdoll*` rules), 2 makes it vanish. Like `hunter_break` it is the client's own setting, since nothing the server simulates reads it.

`tools/shot.sh --view=dead [--victim=prop --as=<id>] [--ph-prop-death=1] --seconds=<t>` renders each; `headless_run` checks the pick, a person lying on the floor, the burst and the topple, and DEATH with and without a fall; `dedicated` checks the cvars.

## Sides, points and stats

`DotTeamRotation` (dot-team) draws the hunters: RANDOM (never the same person twice running when there is anybody else), QUEUE (everybody an equal share over time) or SWAP (last round's props hunt). `!hunter` / `!prop` / `!any` are wishes the draw honours where it can. Points: winning side 10, a find 5, surviving 5, taunts, and a point a second for a prop that shows its face (Q, `reveal_points_per_second`, paid for at most `reveal_paid_seconds`, then a `reveal_cooldown`). `PhProgress` keeps fourteen stats and eight achievements through dot-stats and dot-achievements, keyed by the player's TMC profile, and reports them when `report_progress` is on.
